About the data

What this map shows

Where the growing conditions are right for each mushroom species, today and for the next 7 days.

It does not show where mushrooms are. It never says whether a mushroom can be eaten.

Each species has its own needs

Pine mushrooms, oak boletes, field mushrooms, morels and desert truffles all react to different trees, rain, warmth and ground.

So the map runs a separate check for every species.

The general map shows the best result among the species you have switched on.

The score in one line

Conditions= 🌲 Habitat× 💧 Water× 🌡 Temperature× ⏱ Mushroom clock

If any one of these is poor, the result is poor.

Plenty of rain cannot make up for the wrong trees. Perfect trees cannot make up for dry ground.

A gentle season factor is multiplied in too. Outside a species' usual months its score is lowered by up to half, never to zero.

1. Habitat: the right place

  • Partner trees: many of these species live joined to the roots of certain trees (pine, golden oak, kermes oak). No partner tree, no mushroom.
  • Tree cover, altitude, rock type and past fires.
  • Soil: a gentle nudge for species that prefer acid or alkaline ground.

2. Water: is the ground wet enough?

Every 250 m square has a water "bucket".

  • Rain fills it.
  • Sun and wind empty it: faster on sunny south slopes, slower in shade.

The soil sets the bucket size:

  • Clay and rich soil hold water longer, so good conditions last more days.
  • Sandy or stony soil drains fast, so the window closes sooner.

The same rain can give two different results on two different slopes.

3. Temperature: not too hot, not too cold

Each species has its own comfortable soil temperature. Frost stops most of them.

4. The mushroom clock

Mushrooms do not appear the day it rains.

  • After a soaking rain (about 15–20 mm in 3 days), the clock starts.
  • Warm days make it run faster, cold days slower.
  • When it runs out, the window opens for about 10 to 14 days (longer for desert truffles).
  • After a full window, the ground needs rest and a new soaking rain before the next one.

How sure is the forecast?

  • Sure: today and the next 2 days.
  • Likely: 3 to 4 days ahead.
  • Unsure: 5 or more days ahead. Only a rough guide.

Where the data comes from

  • Weather: Open-Meteo (rain and temperature), updated daily. Soil temperature is worked out from air temperature for now.
  • Terrain: Copernicus elevation model (slope, sun and shade).
  • Forests: Copernicus forest type and tree cover maps.
  • Fields and scrub: Copernicus grassland map and CORINE Land Cover.
  • Soil: SoilGrids by ISRIC (water holding, texture, pH), plus the Geological Map of Cyprus.
  • Burned areas: CORINE Land Cover 2018 for now. EFFIS fire maps (with the fire year) are planned.
  • Species needs: published research from Cyprus and other Mediterranean forests.

Honest limits

  • These are best estimates. Species numbers are starting guesses from research. They will improve as we log real trips.
  • Rain is estimated per area, not per slope. A local storm can be missed.
  • The soil data is a computer estimate and Cyprus has few soil samples. Where it is unsure, soil has no effect on the result. Today that is most of the island.
  • Mushrooms are patchy. A "very good" square can still be empty.
  • Fennel king oyster: we have no map of giant fennel, so fields stand in for it.

Nature reserves are closed to picking and always shown as closed.

The law and the forest

Picking is allowed in state forests without a permit, except in nature reserves.

Rakes and tools are banned: up to 1 year in prison, up to €5,000 fine, or both.

No fires in forests or within 2 km of them.

Pick by hand or knife. Put the leaf litter back.

Safety
